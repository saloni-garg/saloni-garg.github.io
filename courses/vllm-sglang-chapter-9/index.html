05 · Queue depth

Queue wait is your

Little’s law cross-check

L = λ × W λ = 15 req/s at goodput (vLLM, Ch. 5) W = mean time in system ≈ 2.8 s (mixed) L ≈ 42 requests in system If observed concurrency is 42 but queue wait is rising, capacity — not demand measurement — is wrong.

08 · Autoscaling signal

Do not autoscale
on GPU utilisation.

Why it fails here

  • Decode saturates HBM bandwidth before SMs: util looks “low” at the TPOT limit.
  • Prefix-cache hits raise throughput with no util change.
  • KV thrash raises util while goodput falls — scaling signal points the wrong way in the diagnosis, even if it adds replicas.

Scale on waiting work

Queue wait measures unmet demand directly, per tenant, in the same units as the SLO it threatens.

scale_out if queue_wait_p95 > 150 ms for 30 s scale_in if queue_wait_p95 < 30 ms for 10 min AND kv_util < 50%

Asymmetric: fast out, slow in. Cold start is ~90 s; oscillation is self-inflicted.

09 · Autoscaling formula

Replicas from goodput,
not from vibes.

replicas_needed = ceil( offered_RPS / goodput_per_replica × headroom ) vLLM FP8 example: offered 52 req/s ÷ 19 req/s per replica × 1.25 = 3.42 → 4 replicas SGLang FP8 example: offered 52 req/s ÷ 20 req/s per replica × 1.25 = 3.25 → 4 replicas Headroom 1.25 is not waste. It covers: · one replica lost (Drill 1) at N=4 → exactly 25% · burst profile at 3× mean for the trace's burst windows, partially · cold-start lag: new capacity arrives ~90 s after the decision
Per-replica goodput

From Ch. 5 harness, per precision. Re-measure on every engine upgrade.

Headroom is a policy

1.15 for batch tenants, 1.25 mixed, 1.4 if N is small or bursts are sharp.

Cap scale-in

Never below the minimum-replica floor from Slide 11.

11 · Minimum replicas

The floor is set by failure,
not by average load.

Floor formula

N_min = ceil( peak_RPS_in_any_90s_window / goodput_per_replica ) + 1 The +1 is the replica you are allowed to lose at peak. At N=2, losing one halves capacity. At N=4, losing one costs 25% — exactly the 1.25 headroom.

What the floor buys

  • Deploys: drain one, keep SLO.
  • Node loss: absorb, then autoscale.
  • Burst + cold-start lag: 90 s of demand is already inside the fleet.

Cost honesty: the floor runs 24×7. It is the largest single line in the cost workbook, larger than any engine delta.

13 · Failure drill 1

Kill a replica
mid-trace.

t = 0

SIGKILL one of 4 replicas at steady 52 req/s. In-flight decodes on it fail.

t + 4 s

LB drains it. Load per survivor +33%. Queue wait 85 → 310 ms.

t + 31 s

Autoscaler fires on queue wait. Replacement starts cold.

t + 68 s

Chat TTFT p95 breaches 800 ms for one window. TPOT holds.

t + 104 s

Replacement warm. Queue drains. Breach window closes.

Client impact

Requests on the dead replica retry once at the gateway. No silent drops if idempotency keys are set.

Budget spent

~9 min of the 43 min monthly budget in one drill.

Lesson

At N=3 the same drill breaches for ~4 min. That is the floor argument, measured.

Mitigations, in order

1. Per-tenant KV quota / priority: chat reserves its pool share.   2. Separate doc replica pool (the Ch. 7 disaggregation logic, at fleet level).   3. Rate-limit doc arrivals to the pool’s prefill budget. Autoscaling alone adds replicas that the doc tenant immediately fills.

17 · Cost model

Dollars per million tokens,
with the method showing.

node cost assumption: $11.60 / hr for 4× H100 node ($2.90 / GPU-hr incl. host, fabric, storage markup — state yours, recompute) $/1M tokens = node_$/hr ÷ ( tok/s_at_SLO × 3600 ÷ 1,000,000 ) Blended tok/s counts input + output tokens on the frozen mixed trace. Per-tenant prices split GPU-seconds, not token counts: chat is decode-heavy (expensive per token), doc is prefill-heavy (cheaper per token).
What dominates

The replica floor and headroom, not the engine gap. Utilisation of provisioned capacity is the cost lever.

What precision does

FP8 raises tok/s at SLO and halves weight-load time. It moves cost ~25%, not 2×, because decode is not the whole step.

What never to do

Divide node cost by peak tok/s. That prices a service nobody can use at SLO.
